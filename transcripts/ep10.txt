Episode 10. The Senior Forward Deployed Engineer role


The Senior Forward Deployed Engineer role at Databricks used to be called Resident Solution Architect. It's a customer-embedded engineer: you sit with a strategic customer and build, deploy and debug production systems end to end, often Databricks Apps. It's more hands-on than an architect role, and the bar is evidence: production outcomes, role clarity, and certification.

What the profile asks for. Seven or more years in data engineering and platforms. Ten or more years of consulting. The Data Engineer Professional certification and required classes. At least six to eight Databricks projects delivered hands-on. Working knowledge of two or more clouds, deep in one. Deep Spark experience, including runtime internals. CI/CD for production. Current knowledge across the breadth of the platform. And performance and scalability optimisation. The core responsibilities: design and build Databricks Apps, front end and back end; deploy and monitor in production; and debug complex issues across the stack. What distinguishes a Forward Deployed Engineer: turning ambiguous requirements into production systems, full-stack delivery from ingestion to the application layer, and working autonomously in high-visibility engagements.

Assess your fit honestly, requirement by requirement, before the interview. Count your consulting years explicitly, including years at service companies delivering for clients, and frame in-house work as serving internal customers. If you're not certified yet, that's usually the biggest gap at senior level, because the profile lists the certification as completed. Book the exam now, and ask the recruiter whether a date before your start is acceptable. And find your standout strength against their list. If you've built production Databricks Apps, lead with it, because it's their first listed responsibility.

Remember the levelling point: Databricks calibrates candidates from Senior down to Associate based on demonstrated capability, not tenure. Decide before the interview whether you'd accept a non-senior offer.

Your project list. They want six to eight Databricks projects, each with your role and one outcome. Write them down before the interview in one line each: the project, what you personally designed, built or led, and a number. For example: an archival lakehouse, architect and delivery lead, with its record count. A metadata-driven load framework, designed and built, with the volume it has loaded. A self-service Databricks App, weeks to production and users. A governance standard and deployment pipeline, and how many builders use it. A tokenisation or privacy product, and the scale it covers. And be careful: a Spark platform on Hadoop is Spark depth, not a Databricks project, so don't count it.

Now the likely questions.

"Why Forward Deployed Engineer, and why Databricks?" "I'm at my best embedded with a team, turning a fuzzy problem into something running in production. That's what I've done in my current role: a self-service app that went from an idea to production in weeks. This role lets me do that with Databricks' most ambitious customers, with direct access to the product teams. And I've been building on Databricks Apps since early on, so I want to be closer to where it's shaped." Swap in your own example and timeline.

"Design a Databricks App for a customer, end to end." Use seven steps. One, discover: who uses it, what decision it supports, data sensitivity, concurrency and latency. Two, the front end: Streamlit, Dash or Gradio for internal tools built fast, or React with FastAPI for a polished product. Three, authentication: user authorization, on-behalf-of, so Unity Catalog applies per user, with the app's service principal only for shared, non-sensitive operations. Four, data access: a SQL warehouse for analytical queries, Lakebase, the managed Postgres, for app state and fast lookups, and never pull large datasets into the app process. Five, long-running work: trigger a job and poll, rather than blocking a web request. Six, config: resources and secrets declared in the app YAML and the bundle, not hard-coded. Seven, ship it: bundles through CI/CD to dev, test and production, with health checks, logs and alerts. Headline to close: "The app is a thin layer over governed data. Heavy work in warehouses and jobs, state in Lakebase, and security in Unity Catalog."

"Walk me through debugging a complex issue across the stack." Headline: "I work from the symptom down through the layers, and change one thing at a time." The layers: the app, with its logs and request timing. Identity: which identity ran the query, token scopes and grants. The query: the query profile and history, scan size, spill, skew and pruning. Compute: warehouse queuing and scaling, cluster events, driver and executor logs. Data: small files, missing clustering, schema changes, and table history. And network: DNS, private endpoints and firewall rules. Then tell one real story, like a tuning win, with the cause, the fix and the percentage improvement.

"How do you deploy and monitor in production?" Bundles through CI/CD as a service principal, the same artefact through every environment. Job run alerts, pipeline event logs, data quality expectations and freshness checks. Lakehouse Monitoring for drift and quality. App logs and health checks, and inference tables and MLflow traces for AI features. System tables for cost and usage. And runbooks, so the customer can own it after you leave.

"The customer wants something Databricks can't do well." Be honest early. Say what the platform does well and where it doesn't, propose the best design even if it includes another tool, and feed the gap back to the product team with a clear use case. Trust matters more than any single sale, and Forward Deployed Engineers are the customer's voice into Databricks.

"How do you work autonomously in a high-visibility engagement?" Agree success criteria with the sponsor in week one, in writing. Show working software weekly. Surface risks early with options. Make reversible decisions yourself, and escalate irreversible ones with a recommendation. Leave the customer able to run it.

Breadth questions to revise, because the role expects current knowledge of the platform: Lakebase, Agent Bricks, Genie, Lakeflow Connect, Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines, Model Serving, serverless compute, predictive optimization, liquid clustering, Unity Catalog attribute-based access control and metric views, and Delta Sharing. For each, know what it is in one sentence and one situation where you'd use it.

And the Spark internals refresher: Catalyst, whole-stage code generation and Photon, stages at shuffle boundaries, adaptive query execution, unified memory and spill, and the three join strategies: broadcast hash, shuffle hash and sort-merge.

Finally, your profile, per Databricks' own guide. They favour measurable outcomes, clarity about what you led versus what the team did, certifications with dates, a GitHub link, and no more than two pages. And they explicitly penalise generic, AI-sounding wording. So in the interview, use plain verbs precisely. Say "I designed", "I built", "I led", and mean each one, because they'll test it.
